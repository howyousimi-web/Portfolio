Research papers

Break The Light

A three-part research series on blue light exposure, sleep quality, and anxiety in students — from interviews, to a comparative survey, to a working intervention.

Qualitative December 2024 · Livingstone International School

The Influence of Blue Light Exposure on Sleep Quality and Anxiety in STEM Students Ranging 15 to 16

Semi-structured interviews and open-ended surveys explored how 15–16-year-old STEM students understand and cope with blue light from their devices. The study found a clear gap between knowing the risks and actually changing screen habits, pointing to the need for education, parental guidance, and supportive tools.

Research paper · PDF Read paper
Quantitative February 2025 · Livingstone International School

Comparative Study on Blue Light Exposure in Grade 11 Students from Livingstone International School, National University Clark, and Angeles University Foundation

A structured survey of 66 Grade 11 students across three schools, using the GAD-7 anxiety scale and the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index. The null hypothesis was rejected: scores differed significantly by school, with the highest severe-anxiety scores at Livingstone, the poorest sleep at Angeles University Foundation, and the healthiest results at National University.

Research paper · PDF Read paper
Mixed methods 2025 · Livingstone International School

Break The Light: A Pop-Up Platform to Minimize Blue Light Effects for Student Wellness

Building on the two earlier studies, this paper develops and evaluates a pop-up break web platform with customizable reminders, alarms, and digital notepads. Built with an Agile process and an experimental design, it combines baseline GAD-7 and PSQI data with Likert ratings, expert evaluation, and thematic analysis of open-ended feedback from 35 student testers, and found the platform highly acceptable.

Ethical Case Study

Ethics · Group case study October 2026 · National University Clark

Children’s Data and Social Media: How Much Responsibility Should Platforms Have for Protecting Minors’ Privacy and Digital Footprints?

A group case study with a survey of 30 young Filipino internet users, who revealed a paradox: 86.7% say lying about age online is wrong, yet 83.3% have done it. Weighing this against UN General Comment No. 25 and Philippine bills on child online safety, it argues that platforms should adopt Privacy and Safety by Design, device-level age signaling through zero-knowledge proofs, and local legal representation instead of blanket bans.
